# SAQI voiceover scripts

For a recorded human voice, create each clip in ElevenLabs (elevenlabs.io › Text to Speech). Use a warm narrator voice such as **Lily** with the **Eleven v4** model. Download each clip as an MP3 and name it as shown below.

Then put all 8 files in a folder called `audio/` next to `index.html`. The site finds them and plays them on its own, and the toolbar shows **● Recorded voice**. Without the files, the site uses the best voice built into the browser.

## audio/intro.mp3

Hi, and welcome to SAQI. It means the water-bearer. Right now, most desert communities only find out a well is failing when it's already dry. And by then, it's too late. SAQI warns them weeks earlier. On this map, you're looking at six real water sources, across Al Dhafra and Al Ain, in Abu Dhabi. Every night, we combine satellite data, well sensors, and reports from herders. We predict how long each source has left, and we're honest about how sure we are. Then we send a simple Arabic text, and a phone call, to the people who need to act. Go ahead, pick any location, and I'll walk you through it.

## audio/liwa.mp3 (Hameem Well Field)

Let's start with Hameem, in Liwa Oasis. This one's urgent. The water table here is dropping almost forty centimetres, every single week. And it's not just the sensor saying that. The satellites show the aquifer draining, and the date palms are losing their green. When two independent signals agree like this, we trust it. Our forecast? About nineteen days before the pumps run dry, give or take a week. So twelve hundred people have already had a text message and a phone call, in Arabic, telling them to move their herds to Ghayathi from Sunday.

## audio/quaa.mp3 (Al Qua'a Herd Well)

This is Al Qua'a, out near Al Ain. Here, the problem isn't really drought. It's crowding. Herders told us eleven herds came to this one well this week. Last month, it was four. And the satellite pictures show the grazing land around it is basically bare. We think it has about six weeks left. So instead of a scary alarm, the council gets a simple watering rota, and four herds are guided north to Remah, which had good rain last month.

## audio/mz.mp3 (Madinat Zayed Borehole)

Madinat Zayed is a different kind of problem. This well won't run dry. It's turning salty. As the water table drops, deeper, saltier water gets pulled in. Camels can handle salinity up to about twelve, and it's already close to ten. We expect it to cross that line in roughly six weeks. Funny thing is, the herders noticed first. They told us the camels were refusing to drink. And those reports actually made our model more confident.

## audio/sw.mp3 (Sweihan Village Well)

Now, Sweihan. Its sensor went silent six days ago. Probably a flat battery. In the real desert, this happens all the time. So what does SAQI do? It doesn't go blind, and it doesn't panic either. It leans on satellite radar, which can see through cloud, and it asks local herders to text in what they're seeing. The forecast is still around ten weeks, but we widen the range, and we clearly mark it as lower confidence. And a repair crew is already on the way.

## audio/gh.mp3 (Ghayathi Reserve Well)

Ghayathi is one of our healthy wells, with about five months of water to spare. And that matters, because SAQI doesn't just raise alarms. It finds solutions. Ghayathi has spare capacity, so it's where the Hameem herds are being sent. And every week, we check that the extra animals aren't pushing Ghayathi itself into trouble.

## audio/rm.mp3 (Remah Recharge Well)

And finally, some good news. Remah got a big storm in October. Thirty-eight millimetres of rain. The wadi actually flowed, and the water level here is rising. The satellites show the land greening up, by more than twenty percent. So we use this moment. Remah takes four herds from Al Qua'a, and one community's good luck becomes another community's safety net.

## audio/outro.mp3

So, that's SAQI. Satellite and sensor data, an honest prediction, and a warning that reaches a herder's basic phone, weeks before the crisis. Because the first warning shouldn't be an empty bucket.
